# Moro landing page — proposal for approval

Status: approved. The user authorized all sections to be built in parallel.

## Shared direction

Keep the current espresso, caramel, and warm cream palette, editorial serif
headlines, DM Sans interface text, photographic ingredients, and rounded forms.
Backgrounds and hero artwork run edge to edge. Text groups stay within a 1400px
container, with body paragraphs around 55–65 characters wide. This is a coffee
brand concept: prices are illustrative and the bag is a demo, not a checkout.

Motion has three roles: the interactive hero, gentle reveals through the story,
and one memorable closing moment. Avoid pinning every section. Keep native page
scrolling, keyboard controls, reduced-motion support, readable contrast, and
images below 100,000 bytes. The bag remains shared across all menu controls.

## 1. Interactive coffee hero — choose your coffee

Purpose: introduce Moro and make choosing a drink the opening experience.

Retain the full-viewport coffee grounds scene, central cup, curved ingredient
carousel, large headline, current flavor, price, and add-to-bag control. Offer
Original, Mocha, Salted Caramel, Vanilla Cream, Toasted Hazelnut, and Coconut Cream.
Downward scrolling moves the ingredient wheel right; upward scrolling moves it
left. After input pauses, a card settles exactly behind the cup. Only then does
the drink transition. Previous/next buttons and menu selection give direct access.

For the expanded page, add readable navigation links to Menu, Our Story, and
The Ritual, alongside the bag. A visible “Explore the menu” anchor lets visitors
skip the hero interaction. After the final flavor, continued scrolling releases
the hero and reaches the story. The hero must not snap users back from later
sections. On mobile, retain the central cup and side ingredients with compact
controls; keep the menu shortcut easy to reach.

## 2. Brand introduction — a little pause, a better pour

Purpose: give the visual identity a clear, human brand idea.

Transition into warm cream with dark espresso text. Use a large headline and a
short paragraph on the left; a generous close-up of a hand holding an iced coffee
in soft daylight on the right. Suggested copy direction: “For slow starts,
small breaks, and the moments in between.” Follow with a short explanation of
Moro's flavor-led coffee concept and a “Meet the menu” anchor.

Reveal the headline, body, and image in a restrained sequence as they enter view.
Keep the image crop stationary once visible. On mobile, headline and copy come
first, followed by the photo. Do not invent certifications, sourcing locations,
or production claims.

## 3. The full menu — find your everyday favorite

Purpose: let visitors compare and select all six drinks without replaying the
hero interaction.

Create a six-card grid on a slightly deeper cream surface: three columns on
desktop, two on tablet, and one on narrow phones. Each card has its own cup image,
flavor name, concise tasting description, price, and accessible “Add to bag”
button. Proposed tasting directions are bold/smooth for Original, chocolate-rich
for Mocha, buttery/salty for Caramel, mellow/creamy for Vanilla, roasted/nutty for
Hazelnut, and coconut/coffee for Coconut Cream. These are concept descriptions.

Use a small image lift and soft shadow on hover, with an equivalent focus state.
Adding a drink gives a short confirmation and updates the existing bag. Quantity,
removal, and subtotal controls work identically regardless of where it was added.
No filters are necessary for six products. Do not invent nutrition or allergen
guarantees; recipe details must be confirmed before presenting them as facts.

## 4. The craft — from bean to first sip

Purpose: introduce preparation through a visual story.

Use an espresso-colored section with three large numbered scenes: “The beans,”
“The brew,” and “The pour.” Pair macro bean photography, coffee extraction, and
milk meeting coffee with one short paragraph each. A fine connecting line and
consistent image crops make the sequence easy to follow. Copy describes the
concept's intended preparation, without invented brewing times or origin claims.

Let the line draw and the three scenes reveal as the section enters view. Keep
the page moving normally; this section does not pin or take over scrolling.
Stack the three steps vertically on mobile, with the same reading order.

## 5. Everyday rituals — made for your kind of pause

Purpose: show the drink as part of someone's day and add warmth beyond product
photography.

Build an editorial photo composition around three moments: a slow morning by a
window, an afternoon coffee at a desk, and a cup carried on a walk. Use one large
photo and two smaller complementary crops, with short captions and an oversized
headline. Avoid testimonial ratings or customer quotes without real sources.

Use small, one-time photo reveals. A “Find your flavor” link returns to the full
menu. Mobile becomes a simple vertical photo story; captions stay outside the
images for reliable readability.

## 6. Closing invitation — your next good pause starts here

Purpose: give visitors a clear next step after the brand story.

Use a spacious warm cream section with a large centered headline, one line of
supporting copy, and a small arrangement of three coffee cups below it. The main
button, “Choose your coffee,” links to the full menu. A secondary “View your bag”
button opens the existing cart and shows the current item count.

Bring the cups into place with a subtle stagger on first entry, then leave them
still. Avoid continuous bobbing. On phones, reduce the artwork and keep the main
button immediately visible. No newsletter form or checkout is implied without
an actual service behind it.

## 7. Signature footer — one last taste of Moro

Purpose: finish with an identifiable brand moment and useful navigation.

Return to dark espresso with an oversized cream “moro.” wordmark spanning the
available width. Above it, place compact links to Menu, Our Story, and The Ritual,
plus the bag and a back-to-top control. Include a short concept credit. Social,
contact, and legal links should appear only with approved destinations or be
clearly marked as placeholders.

On first entry, let a subtle coffee-ring outline expand behind the wordmark while
the letters settle into place in a short stagger. A circular back-to-top
button lifts slightly on hover or keyboard focus and scrolls to the start when
activated. No automatic looping. Reduced motion shows the finished composition
immediately. Mobile stacks the links above the wordmark without clipping them.

## Approval and build order

The user approved this structure and authorized all sections to be built in parallel.
All seven sections are integrated into index.html; the shared cart and navigation
connect the full page.
